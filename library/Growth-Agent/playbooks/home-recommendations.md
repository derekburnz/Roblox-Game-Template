# Playbook: Roblox Home recommendations

Home (the "Recommended For You" rows) is how a Roblox game grows without paying for every player. This is what moves it and how to work on it. Sources are named at the end. Roblox changes this system, so re-read the official pages before a big decision.

## How it works
- **Two stages.** Retrieval picks a set of games a player might like. Ranking orders them. Ranking depends on how players who arrived from Home behave.
- **Explore, then expand.** A new game is shown to a small group first. If that group plays, stays and comes back, it is shown to similar groups. A content update is one known trigger for a fresh look. There is no published recovery time and no permanent ban.
- **Signals are per-player averages,** not totals. A small game with strong numbers can outrank a big one with weak numbers.
- **Ads do not count.** Players first acquired from ads are left out of ranking. Ads can help a game get considered in retrieval, and they are a way to buy test players for your funnels. Nothing more.

## The signals, in the order to fix them
1. **Tap rate (play-through rate):** of the people shown the tile, how many press play. Moved by the icon, the thumbnails and the title. This gates everything: great retention with a weak tile still gets no impressions.
2. **Quitting in the first minute:** the share who leave in under 60 seconds. Moved by load time and the first 20 seconds.
3. **Quitting between 1 and 3 minutes:** the share who leave at 61 to 180 seconds. Moved by whether the game is what the tile promised and whether the next goal is clear.
4. **Coming back:** day 1, days 2 to 7 and days 8 to 28.
5. **Playtime** per player per day.
6. **Playing with friends** (days played together on purpose).
7. **Spending** (days with a purchase and amount per player).

Roblox's own advice: start with your weakest signal. Retention matters most, then monetization, and both matter only if players press play and stay.

## Where to read them
Creator Hub > Analytics > Acquisition > the Home recommendations tab. Each number has a benchmark against similar games. Read these two benchmarks: your genre and "experiences with similar players". Acquisition data lags about two days.

## The promise rule (no clickbait)
The tile is a promise about the first minute. A tile that wins the tap and loses the player in the first minute now hurts the game twice. Before any icon, thumbnail, title or first description line:
1. List what a new player really sees and does in their first three minutes.
2. Keep only promises on that list. Lead with the real core action.
3. No rare or late-game hero art unless a new player really sees it early.
4. No invented numbers, odds or money amounts on a picture.

## Icon and thumbnails
- One clear subject with a readable face or action. Show the feeling and the one mechanic that makes the game different.
- Check at phone size: about 150 px wide for a thumbnail and about 50 px for the icon. If it does not read, make the subject bigger.
- Three words at most on a picture, huge and high contrast. A picture named after a feature does worse than one that shows an action.
- Never crop the main character. Do not copy another game's layout.
- Home thumbnails can be tested: upload a set and Roblox rotates them. Give each about 1,000 impressions before judging, then replace the worst. One new thumbnail every 4 to 5 days at most; tap rate decays, so keep one spare ready.
- Upload order: detail page pictures first, then the Home set, then the icon last (it changes the tile everywhere). Log the upload minute so the read starts from it.
- The store page video matters: real gameplay, the best moment in the first 3 seconds.
- Every upload waits for the owner's OK on a contact sheet. Moderation can take minutes to hours.

## The first minute
- A new player does the fun thing the name promises inside 20 seconds.
- No social asks, shop prompts or like requests in the first minutes.
- Fewer pop-ups. One instruction at a time.
- Measure load time by device. Players leave while loading.

## Working rhythm
Fix the worst signal, ship it, wait for the data window, read, repeat. More ads without a better game does nothing. Judge a first-session fix only after a clean week on a steady audience.

## A good sign and a bad sign
- Good: Home shows the game to more people each day for several days and the tap rate holds near the benchmark.
- Bad: impressions fall day after day. That means the group Roblox tried did not respond. It is a failed test, not a ban. The fix is a better tile and first minute, then a trigger such as a real content update.

## Featured programs
Being featured brings a wider, less targeted audience, so per-player numbers drop at once. Plan for it; it is still worth having.
