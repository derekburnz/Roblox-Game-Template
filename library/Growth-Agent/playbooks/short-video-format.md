# Playbook: short video format

Rules for TikTok, Reels and Shorts videos about the game, and for the game page video. They come from studying short videos that spread, from a scripting method (write for one viewer, each line sells the next) and from the owner's notes on the first story videos. Copy the format of videos that work, never their art, music or words.

The reasons behind these rules, from a study of videos that spread, are in `reels-study.md` beside this file.

## What videos that spread all do
1. **The payoff is on screen in the first second.** No greeting, no logo card, no slow establishing shot.
2. **One idea per beat, about 3 to 5 seconds each, with a visible counter** ("2/5"), so the viewer always knows how far along they are.
3. **Big subtitles, word by word, in the middle of the frame,** bold, with the key word coloured. Every line has a picture. The video works on mute.
4. **A narrator character in the corner,** the same one every time. It builds recognition without anyone showing their face.
5. **Short:** aim for 25 to 40 seconds. Cut anything with no visual change in the next 3 seconds.
6. **An ending that asks for something small.** A question gets more comments than "follow us".

## The story format
The format that worked best for a game: a short narrated story about one player, in the style of a current trend.
- **Open with the trend's line,** spoken over the most striking shot. Check what the trend's opener is right now and match its wording and voice style.
- **Script rules:** write for one viewer (for example a 9 to 14 year old who likes rare things and showing off). Line 1 opens a question with a specific object or number. The first payoff lands by about 8 seconds. Open a second question at 60 to 70% of the way through. Lines under 9 words, about one cut per line. Answer the one objection the video raises in a single line.
- **Honest odds and money:** odds appear only as the real in-game number. Never "first try", never "free", never a Robux price.
- **The end card is the game icon plus a join line, and nothing else.** No gameplay clip tacked on the end: the viewer goes to the page to see more.
- **Every shot shows something happening** and reads on a phone. No sky-only shots, no dark scenes, no character standing still. A character must never clip through the scenery.

## Layout (1080 x 1920, 30 fps)
- The video fills the screen.
- The headline sits over the top on a soft dark fade.
- The caption sits in the lower middle.
- The narrator is a small overlay in a bottom corner, not half the screen.
- Hide the game's side menus before recording, and crop the HUD out of gameplay shots.

## Captions
No long descriptions. A caption is:
1. The game's name with "on Roblox".
2. The link.
3. Three to five plain search words (also put them in the alt text). Plain words now do more than hashtags.
4. Tags, as many as each app allows without penalty. Check each app's current limit; going over can void them all.

## Testing hooks
- **Trial reels:** where an app offers it, post two or three versions of the same video with different openers to non-followers first, and put only the winner on the main page. The owner picks the winner.
- Try each tone (cozy, funny, rare pull) at least three times before judging it.
- Read results a few days after posting: views, likes, comments and follows per platform, in one table.

## Young audience
- A comment question that fits kids ("Which one would you pick first?") is fine.
- Never "comment X and I will message you the link". That is direct-message bait, and the audience is young.
- No player names on screen.

## Voice
- A free local text-to-speech voice is fine as the default. Pick one voice and keep it for every line and every video.
- A paid voice needs the owner's OK, and its plan must allow commercial use. Free tiers often do not.
- Voice over real pictures beats plain footage with music.

## Music and sound
Only the game's own music or music licensed for use off Roblox. Music from Roblox's Creator Store is licensed for use inside Roblox only.

## The game page video
Different rules, because it sits on the store page:
- Real gameplay only.
- No on-screen text.
- Smooth takes (see `library/Motion-FX-Agent/video-tools/recording-tips.md`).
- The test character is not the focus, and no clutter in frame (pop-ups, tips, other players' names).
- The best moment in the first 3 seconds. About 20 seconds, 1920 x 1080.
- The owner uploads it.

## How we watched other people's videos
Some sites block cloud threads. What works: the owner's signed-in browser, with the owner's say, opening the video and sampling frames at a few timestamps to read the hook, the on-screen text, the pacing and the ending. Never log in and never type anything. Third-party tools that promise to do this are read before they are installed, and most need a login cookie that must not be handed over.
