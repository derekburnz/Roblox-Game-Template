# Playbook: the go or no-go read (template)

A go or no-go read answers one question for the owner: keep putting time and money into this game, or stop. It is the most important file Growth writes, so it is done on Opus 5.5 and saved in the library (it holds money numbers, so never in a public repo).

Why write it: without a written rule, every week's numbers look like a reason to try one more thing.

## Shape of the file

### Verdict
One or two sentences, first. Then the decision day.

### What the numbers say
A small table against Roblox's benchmarks, then numbered plain-language points. Use round counts ("of 100 new players, ____ are still there at 2 minutes").

| | Us, dates ____ | Typical | Top tenth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Come back next day (D1) | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Come back a week later (D7) | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Minutes per player per day | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Press play when Roblox shows it | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Players who buy something | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Robux per buyer | ____ | ____ | ____ |

"Typical" and "Top tenth" are the genre median and 90th percentile from the Creator Hub benchmark tiles on the day of the read. Write the genre and date: ____.

Cover in the points:
1. How fast players leave (2, 5, 10 and 30 minutes).
2. Whether it differs by device and by source (ads, friends, Home).
3. What Home is doing (impressions per day and the tap rate).
4. Whether the game has players when ads are off.
5. Money: spent on ads, earned, cost per ad player against earnings per new player.
6. Which ads bought which players (minutes played per ad player, cost each). The cheapest players often leave soonest.

### Why not call it dead today
The honest case for continuing: the best days in the data and what was different about them, what returning players do, technical health.

### Why it may still be a no
The honest case against: what stays low even for the easiest audience, how much of the game most players never reach.

### The test
- **Stop now:** what to pause so the test is clean (ad changes, the flood of merges, new content).
- **Fix only these:** at most three changes, each tied to one number, with a date.
- **Clean window:** a full week on one version and a steady audience. Dates ____ to ____. Read D1 and playtime the day after it ends and D7 a few days later.

### What would change the verdict
Thresholds, written before the test and not moved.

| | Now | Keep going | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Come back next day | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Minutes per player | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Still playing at 5 minutes | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Come back a week later | ____ | ____ | ____ |

- **Keep going** if the main two both clear the line.
- **Stop** if both are still under the stop line after a clean week on a steady audience. Say what carries over to a next game: the pipeline, the funnels, the lessons.
- **In between:** one more round, only if both moved at least halfway to the line. No third round.
- A bonus sign either way: Roblox showing the game on Home again without more ad money.

### What I am unsure about
Small samples, lagging tiles, things that changed on the same day and cannot be told apart.

### Sources
Every page read, with the date.

## Rules for the read
- A clean week needs one version of the game. If hundreds of changes merged in the same week as the numbers, say that the numbers cannot tell a game that does not work from a game that was tested badly.
- Keep ad spend steady during a test. A change in ad audience changes every number.
- Give a judgment where there is no measurement, and label it as a judgment.
- Send the owner the verdict in a few plain lines with one simple chart, and save it to memory.
