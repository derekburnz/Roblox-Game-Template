# Playbook: reels study

What a study of short videos that spread taught about format, and why each rule holds. Read it before planning any short video or post. The rules themselves live in `short-video-format.md`; this file keeps the reasons. Copy the format of videos that work, never their art, music or words.

## How to watch other people's videos
- Some sites block cloud threads, and their robots rules refuse web fetch. Third party tools that promise to read them often need a login cookie and cannot open a single video. Read a tool's code before installing it, and never hand over a login cookie.
- What works: the owner's signed-in browser, only when the owner says so. Open the video, expand the caption, then pause the video with a script and step to a few timestamps (the first fraction of a second, then every few seconds to the end), taking a close screenshot each time. That shows the hook, the on-screen text, the pacing and the ending. The voice is read from the burned-in subtitles. Never log in and never type anything.

## What the videos that spread all do, and why
1. **The payoff is on screen in the first second.** A big number, a before and after, or the most striking moment. No greeting, no logo card, no slow opener. Why: viewers decide to stay or skip in that first second.
2. **A new beat every 3 to 5 seconds, with a visible counter** such as "2/5". Why: the viewer always knows how far along they are and how much is left, which keeps watch time up.
3. **Big subtitles, word by word, in the middle of the frame,** bold, with one key word coloured. Every line has a picture. Why: most people watch on mute, and a moving word keeps the eye on the frame.
4. **A recurring character in the corner,** the same one in every video. Why: it builds recognition without anyone showing their face.
5. **25 to 40 seconds.** Cut anything with no visual change in the next 3 seconds. Why: average watch time matters more than views, and shorter videos are watched closer to the end.
6. **An ending that asks a small question.** Why: videos that end on a comment prompt get far more comments than ones that end on "follow us". For a young audience, use a kid friendly question ("Which one would you pick first?"). Never "comment a word and I will message you the link": that is direct message bait.
7. **Captions that work without sound.** Why: many viewers read the caption before or instead of listening.

## Tips the videos repeat (claims, not tested)
- **Plain search words beat hashtags.** Put 3 to 5 plain words that people search for in the caption and in the alt text.
- **Watch time and skip rate matter more than views.** A big drop in the first seconds is the thing to fix.
- **Trial reels:** where an app offers it, post two or three versions of the same video with different openers to non-followers first, and put only the winner on the main page. The owner picks the winner.
- **Voice over real pictures beats plain footage with music.**

## What to do with it
- Open on the best moment, not the opener. If a trend has a set first line, say it over the most striking shot. Test a version that opens on a big number.
- Put a progress counter on screen for any list or set of steps.
- Pick one of the game's own characters as the corner narrator and keep it the same every time.
- Word by word subtitles in the centre, in the game's own colours, with one coloured word.
- Aim for 25 to 40 seconds.
- End card: the game icon, a join line and a kid friendly comment question.
- Use trial reels to test two or three hooks for the same video. Posts still go out only on the owner's say.
- Captions: the game's name, the link, 3 to 5 plain search words and tags.
